Yes. A sphere prior can help a lot.

Since your glass particles are nearly spherical, you can use that fact in three places to label them better:

1. Better marker detection

For spheres, the distance-transform peak should be near the center of each bead.

From your numbers:

mean diameter ≈ 1757 µm
voxel size = 24.766 µm

So the bead diameter is about:

~71 voxels
radius ~35 voxels

That means one real bead should usually produce one main distance peak within about that scale.

What this changes

Your current setting:

MARKER_MIN_DIST = 21

is probably a bit small for ~71-voxel spheres, so one bead can produce multiple peaks.

A better test range is:

MARKER_MIN_DIST = 28
# then test 30, 32, maybe 35

Also smooth the distance map before peak_local_max:

dist = ndimage.distance_transform_edt(glass_mask)
dist_smooth = ndimage.gaussian_filter(dist, sigma=1.0)

coords = peak_local_max(
    dist_smooth,
    min_distance=30,
    threshold_abs=8.0,
    labels=glass_mask.astype(np.uint8),
    exclude_border=False
)

bead_labels = watershed(-dist_smooth, markers, mask=glass_mask)

That is the easiest way to use the “they are spheres” knowledge.

2. Shape-based cleanup after labeling

Because beads should be spherical, you can identify suspicious labels:

very elongated object → probably merged beads
very tiny fragment → over-segmentation/noise
object touching wall with distorted shape → likely wall-connected artifact

So after watershed, compute for each label:

equivalent diameter
bounding box ratios
true sphericity or at least compactness
touches wall: yes/no

Then flag labels such as:

diameter much larger than expected bead size
bbox ratio far from 1
low sphericity
wall-touching + distorted

Those are the labels to inspect or split further.

3. Use sphere size as a constraint

Because your beads are close to one size, you can use the expected diameter range as a rule.

From your D10–D90:

D10 ≈ 1690 µm
D90 ≈ 1818 µm

That is about:

68 to 73 vox diameter

So after labeling:

one object much bigger than this may actually be two merged beads
one object much smaller may be fragment/noise

This gives you a strong QC filter.
