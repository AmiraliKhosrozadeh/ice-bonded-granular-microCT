\section{Ice-path maps of every specimen}
Figures~\ref{fig:tau_glass}--\ref{fig:tau_sand} give the map of Fig.~18 of
the paper for every specimen.  For every ice voxel the shortest path
through the ice from the support face is computed with a cost inversely
proportional to the local ice fraction in a \SI{0.5}{\milli\meter} window
(normalised by the part of the window inside the grain envelope, so the
lateral surface does not read as porous).  The support face is treated as
an equal-potential boundary, as in the diffusion solve: every ice voxel
that is the first of its axial line from the support, within
\SI{3}{\milli\meter} of it, seeds the path, and the axial distance is
measured from that seed surface.  The ratio of path to axial distance is
divided by its median at the same height in the unloaded column of the same
specimen, and ice above the 99.5th percentile of the unloaded column at the
same height (bodies of at least \SI{0.5}{\milli\meter\cubed}) is drawn
solid and coloured, the remaining ice as a faint shell, so an unloaded
column is blank by construction and a loaded one shows only what exceeds
the unloaded range.  The map is computed inside the grain envelope on the
largest 26-connected ice body, on the $2\times$-binned phase maps for the
beads and the full-resolution phase maps for the sand, whose envelope is
pulled in by \SI{1}{\milli\meter} to leave out the ice-rich skin the sand
packs against the tube wall; the \SI{6}{\milli\meter} next to the support
face are cut, and a line separates the unloaded scan from the loaded ones.
